Stems in λ μ ν ρ: the future adds ε (not σ) and contracts like ποιέω. The aorist drops σ and lengthens the stem vowel (ε → ει, α → η/ᾱ). So ἀγγελῶ / ἤγγειλα, ἀποκτενῶ / ἀπέκτεινα, βαλῶ, and Attic κομιῶ.

Notes

Tense vs. aspect

Only the indicative shows time, so only it takes an augment. Present stem = ongoing; aorist stem = a simple whole act. παιδεῦσαι = “to teach,” not “to have taught.”

Voice

Present & imperfect: middle = passive in form. ὑπό + gen. → passive; an accusative object → middle. Future & aorist forms here are middle only.

Uses of αὐτός

  • Oblique cases alone: πέμπω αὐτόν, I send him.
  • Predicate position: αὐτὸς ὁ ῥήτωρ, the orator himself.
  • Attributive: ὁ αὐτὸς ῥήτωρ, the same orator.

Reflexives

Refer back to the subject: παιδεύω ἐμαυτόν, I teach myself. For possession, put the reflexive in attributive position: τὸν ἑαυτοῦ ἵππον, his own horse.

Demonstratives

With a noun they stand in predicate position, and the noun keeps its article: οὗτος ὁ λόγος, this word. οὗτος points back; ὅδε points ahead; ἐκεῖνος = that.

Augment

Consonant → ἐ-. Vowel → lengthen (α, ε → η; ο → ω). Compounds augment after the prefix (ἀπέπεμπον). ἔχω → εἶχον.

Perfect tense L20

A completed action with a lasting result: γέγραφα τὴν ἐπιστολήν, “I have written the letter (and it’s written).” Pluperfect = the same, set in the past. Perfect stem = reduplication + stem + κ (1st perfect) or no κ (2nd perfect).

Contraction

  • α + ο/ω → ω; + ε/η → ᾱ; ι-diphthongs keep ι as subscript
  • ε + ε → ει; ε + ο → ου; ε vanishes before a long vowel
  • ο + ε/ο/ου → ου; + η/ω → ω; + ι-diphthong → οι

Time expressions L23

Gen. = time within which (τῆς νυκτός, during the night). Dat. = time when, often with ἐν (ἐκείνῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ). Acc. = extent of time (πολλὰ ἔτη, for many years).

Nominative

Subject

The doer of the verb. Often left out because the ending already shows the subject.

ὁ δοῦλος φεύγει.The slave is fleeing.

Predicate nominative

After εἰμί, a noun or adjective describing the subject is also nominative. The subject usually has the article; the predicate usually doesn't.

ὁ νεανίας μαθητής ἐστιν.The young man is a student.

Genitive

Possession

“Of” or ’s. Usually in attributive position.

ἡ τοῦ δεσπότου οἰκίαthe master’s house

Agent

ὑπό + gen. names the person who does a passive action.

ὁ ἵππος λύεται ὑπὸ τοῦ οἰκέτου.The horse is being released by the servant.

Separation / source

Motion away from, with ἐκ, ἀπό, or after ἐλεύθερος.

ἐλεύθεροι κινδύνουfree from danger

With adjectives

ἄξιος, ἀνάξιος, αἴτιος take a genitive: worthy of, responsible for.

ἄξιος τιμῆςworthy of honor

With ἀκούω

The person heard goes in the genitive; the thing heard can be accusative.

ἀκούομεν τοῦ ῥήτορος.We hear the orator.

χάριν + gen.

“For the sake of.” χάριν is postpositive: it comes after its genitive.

τῆς εἰρήνης χάρινfor the sake of peace

Dative

Indirect object

“To” or “for” someone.

πέμπω δῶρον τῇ ἀδελφῇ.I send a gift to my sister.

Possession

ἐστί(ν) + dative of the owner = “X has.” The thing owned is the nominative subject.

ἔστι τῷ φύλακι ἀσπίς.The guard has a shield.

Means / instrument

“With, by means of” a thing. No preposition. Compare the agent, which is a person with ὑπό.

βάλλουσι τοὺς πολεμίους λίθοις.They hit the enemy with stones.

With verbs

δουλεύω, πείθομαι (obey), χαίρω (rejoice in), ἐπιπλήττω, προσβάλλω take a dative object.

οἱ δοῦλοι πείθονται τῷ δεσπότῃ.The slaves obey the master.

With adjectives

φίλος, ἐχθρός, πολέμιος: dear to, hateful to, at war with.

φίλος τοῖς θεοῖςdear to the gods

χάριν ἔχειν + dat.

“Be grateful to.”

χάριν ἔχω σοι.I am grateful to you.

Accusative

Direct object

The thing or person acted on.

τὴν ἐπιστολὴν γράφω.I am writing the letter.

Subject of an infinitive

After κελεύω, πείθω, impersonal ἔστι, ὥρα: the one who does the infinitive is accusative.

κελεύω σε μένειν.I order you to stay.

Double accusative

ἐρωτάω takes the person asked and the thing asked about.

ἐρωτῶ σε τὸ ὄνομα.I ask you your name.

Adverbial accusative

A neuter accusative used as an adverb: μόνον only, πολύ / πολλά much.

πολλὰ χαίρει.He rejoices much.

Vocative

Direct address

Usually with ὦ, which is not translated or is “O.”

ὦ φίλε, ἄκουε.Listen, friend.

Article & adjectives

Attributive position

Directly after an article, the adjective describes the noun.

ὁ ἀγαθὸς λόγος / ὁ λόγος ὁ ἀγαθόςthe good word

Predicate position

Outside the article–noun group, the adjective makes a statement. Supply “is.”

ὁ λόγος ἀγαθός.The word is good.

Substantive

Article + adjective, adverb, phrase or genitive acts as a noun. Gender shows what to supply.

οἱ ἀγαθοί · τὰ κακά · οἱ νῦν · οἱ ἐν τῇ ἀγορᾷgood men · evil things · people of today · the men in the marketplace

ὁ μέν … ὁ δέ

The article + μέν / δέ as a pronoun: “the one … the other,” “some … others.”

οἱ μὲν φεύγουσιν, οἱ δὲ μένουσιν.Some flee, others stay.

Neuter plural subject

Takes a singular verb.

τὰ δῶρα καλά ἐστιν.The gifts are beautiful.

Two-ending adjectives

No separate feminine; the masculine forms serve for both.

ἡ ἀθάνατος θεόςthe immortal goddess

Pronouns

αὐτός: pronoun

Gen./dat./acc. without the article = him, her, it, them.

πέμπω αὐτόν.I send him.

αὐτός: intensive

Predicate position, or alone in the nominative = -self.

αὐτὸς ὁ ῥήτωρthe orator himself

αὐτός: same

Attributive position = same.

ὁ αὐτὸς ῥήτωρthe same orator

Demonstratives

Predicate position, and the noun keeps its article. ἐκεῖνος … οὗτος = the former … the latter.

οὗτος ὁ ἵππος · ὁ ἵππος ἐκεῖνοςthis horse · that horse

Reflexive

Refers back to the subject. No nominative.

παιδεύω ἐμαυτόν.I teach myself.

Reflexive possession

Reflexive in attributive position = one’s own. Compare αὐτοῦ (predicate position) = someone else’s.

τὸν ἑαυτοῦ ἵππον λύει. · τὸν ἵππον αὐτοῦ λύει.He frees his own horse. · He frees his (another man’s) horse.

Personal pronoun possession

Unemphatic genitive in predicate position; emphatic form in attributive position.

ὁ φίλος μου · ὁ ἐμοῦ φίλοςmy friend · MY friend

Emphatic nominative

The nominative pronoun is used only for emphasis or contrast.

ἐγὼ μὲν γράφω, σὺ δὲ φεύγεις.I am writing, but you are fleeing.

Verbs & infinitives

Complementary infinitive

Completes the verb: ἐθέλω, σπεύδω, φιλέω (be fond of), ἔχω (be able).

ἐθέλω γράφειν.I am willing to write.

μέλλω + infinitive

+ future infin. = be about to, intend to; + present infin. = delay, hesitate.

μέλλει πέμψειν τὴν ἐπιστολήν.He is about to send the letter.

Accusative + infinitive

κελεύω (acc. or dat.) and πείθω + acc. + infin.

πείθομεν τοὺς φίλους μένειν.We persuade our friends to stay.

Impersonal ἔστι / ὥρα

ἔστι + acc. + infin. = it is possible; ὥρα (ἐστί) + acc./dat. + infin. = it is time.

ὥρα ἡμῖν φεύγειν.It is time for us to flee.

ἄξιος + infinitive

Worthy to, deserving to.

ἄξιός ἐστι τιμᾶσθαι.He is worthy to be honored.

Aspect in infinitives

Present vs. aorist infinitive both = “to ___.” The difference is ongoing vs. simple action, not time.

κελεύω σε λῦσαι τὸν ἵππον.I order you to free the horse (once).

Middle / passive

Pres. & impf. share forms. ὑπό + gen. → passive; an object → middle. Deponents (ἔσομαι, ἀκούσομαι, φεύξομαι) are middle in form, active in meaning.

λύομαι τὸν ἵππον.I ransom the horse.

Perfect & pluperfect

A completed action whose result still holds. The pluperfect puts that state in the past.

γέγραφα τὴν ἐπιστολήν.I have written the letter (it is written).

“Has been killed”

ἀποκτείνω is always active. For the passive idea (be killed, die) Greek uses ἀποθνῄσκω.

ὁ κλὼψ ἀπέκτεινε τὸν φύλακα.The thief killed the guard.

Commands & negation

Imperatives

2nd person = command; 3rd person = “let him / them.”

πεμπέτω τὸ δῶρον.Let him send the gift.

Prohibition

μή + imperative.

μὴ κλέπτε.Don’t steal!

οὐ vs. μή

οὐ (οὐκ, οὐχ) with the indicative (facts); μή with imperatives and infinitives. Same split for οὐκέτι / μηκέτι.

οὐκέτι φεύγουσιν.They no longer flee.

Idioms & particles

Faring well or badly

εὖ / κακῶς πράττειν or ἔχειν.

κακῶς πράττει.He is faring badly.

Fixed phrases

τῇ ἀληθείᾳ truly · οἱ πολλοί the many · γελάω ἐπί + dat. laugh at · βλέπω εἰς + acc. look at · μένω + acc. wait for.

γελῶσιν ἐπὶ τῷ κλωπί.They laugh at the thief.

Correlatives

καί … καί both … and · μέν … δέ on the one hand … on the other · ἤ … ἤ either … or · οὐ μόνον … ἀλλὰ καί not only … but also.

οὐ μόνον σοφὸς ἀλλὰ καὶ ἀγαθόςnot only wise but also good

Postpositives

δέ, γάρ, οὖν, μέν, γε never stand first; they usually come second in their clause.

ὁ γὰρ λόγος ἀληθής.For the story is true.

Elision & movable ν

A final short vowel drops before a vowel (ἀπ’, δι’, ἀλλ’); before rough breathing π τ → φ θ (ἀφ’, μεθ’). Movable ν before a vowel or at the end of a sentence.

Time, relatives & L20–23 syntax

Genitive of time within which

Some point within a period, not all of it.

τῆς νυκτὸς φεύγουσιν.They flee during the night.

Dative of time when

The moment an action happens, often with ἐν. The whole day is seen as one point.

ἐκείνῃ τῇ νυκτὶ ἔφυγον.They fled on that night.

Accusative of extent of time

How long: the action fills the whole period.

πολλὰ ἔτη ἔμενεν.He stayed for many years.

Relative clauses

ὅς, ἥ, ὅ agrees with its antecedent in gender and number; its case comes from its own clause.

ὁ λόγος ὃν λέγεις ἀληθής ἐστιν.The story that you are telling is true.

πᾶς and position

Predicate position = all; attributive = whole; no article = every; alone = everyone / all things.

πάντες οἱ ἄνθρωποι · ἡ πᾶσα χώρα · πᾶσα ἡμέραall the people · the whole country · every day

τίς vs. τις

τίς (accented) asks a question; enclitic τις means a certain, some. Neuter τί as an adverb = why?

τίς ἐστιν; · ἄνθρωπός τις · τί φεύγεις;Who is it? · a certain man · Why are you fleeing?

Possessive adjectives

Attributive position, like any adjective.

ὁ ἐμὸς ἵππος · ἡ ἡμετέρα οἰκίαmy horse · our house

τινὲς μὲν … τινὲς δέ

Some … others (compare οἱ μὲν … οἱ δέ). οἱ ἄλλοι = the rest.

τινὲς μὲν φεύγουσι, τινὲς δὲ μένουσιν.Some flee, others stay.

τε

Enclitic “and.” It comes right after the word it joins, usually with καί or another τε for both … and.

ἐγώ τε καὶ σύboth I and you

οὐδέ … οὐδέ / οὔτε … οὔτε

Both mean neither … nor. οὐδέ … οὐδέ joins clauses; οὔτε … οὔτε joins clauses or single words. Alone, οὐδέ = not even. The μη- forms go wherever μή belongs.

οὔτε σοφὸς οὔτε ἀγαθόςneither wise nor good

ἕως vs. ἐπεί

ἕως (while) = same time as the main verb; ἐπεί (after) = earlier, usually with the aorist.

ἕως ἔλεγον, ἠκούομεν.While they were speaking, we were listening.

παρά + gen. of source

Like πρός + gen., the person an action comes from (compare ὑπό + gen. of agent).

ταῦτα παρὰ τοῦ ἀγγέλου ἤκουσα.I heard these things from the messenger.

κατά in idioms

By land / by sea. κατά never marks a personal agent.

κατὰ γῆν καὶ κατὰ θάλαττανby land and by sea

ἀμφί + acc. of followers

The people around someone: his followers or circle.

οἱ ἀμφὶ ΣωκράτηSocrates and his circle

ἀνά / κατά: day by day

Opposites for direction (up / down), but in idioms they can mean the same.

ἀνὰ πᾶσαν ἡμέραν · καθ’ ἡμέρανday by day, daily
